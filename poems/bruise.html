Summary

The speaker reimagines the universe not as creation but as wound—a bruise that bloomed spontaneously on the surface of nothingness. Stars and galaxies become symptoms of injury: fresh pain, mottled terrain, colors that shouldn't exist where there was only void. We inhabit this tender damage, temporary guests within something that swells and will eventually fade.

Themes

This sonnet reframes existence through metaphor of injury and impermanence. Rather than portraying creation as intentional or meaningful, the universe becomes accidental trauma—"impact without a fist, without a why." The extended bruise metaphor transforms cosmic beauty into pathology: violet nebulae and golden stars aren't divine handiwork but discoloration, blood pooling beneath the skin of nothing. Yet there's strange tenderness here—the bruise is "still fresh pain," suggesting we live in the immediate aftermath of existence's beginning, that reality itself is recent and raw. The poem embraces cosmic insignificance not with nihilistic despair but almost gentle acceptance. We're "brief guests / within the throb, the swell, the slow un-pressed"—acknowledging our transience while recognizing we participate in something physically present, felt, real even if accidental. This explores tensions between beauty and violence, accident and meaning, permanence and healing. If the universe is a bruise, it will eventually fade back into the nothing it came from, making our existence both more precious and more absurd.

The Universe Is a Bruise on the Skin of Nothingness

by Krishna Patel

The universe is just a bruise: some swell
of color blooming where there should be none—
a violet ache, a gold the void can't tell
from wound or wonder. See how it was done:

some sudden blow against the skin of dark,
impact without a fist, without a why,
just pressure, and then light—a purple mark
spread thin across the nothing, vast as sky.

And tender still. The stars are still fresh pain,
bright yellow edges where the blood runs new.
Galaxies: the mottled, bruised terrain
that swells and softens into deeper blue.

We live inside the injury—brief guest
within the throb, the swell, the slow un-pressed.

Krishna Patel
