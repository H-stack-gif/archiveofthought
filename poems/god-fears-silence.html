I. God Fears the Silence After Prayer

by Krishna Patel

Summary

This sonnet proposes a radical reversal: God does not answer prayers—rather, prayers create and sustain God. When humanity stops praying, when altars grow cold and tongues fall silent, divinity itself begins to dissolve. God exists only as the sum of human invocation, belief, and repetition.

Themes

The poem explores the dependency relationship between creator and created, inverting traditional theology by suggesting God needs humanity more than humanity needs God. Divinity is portrayed not as an independent, eternal force but as something fragile, held together only by constant human attention and speech. The "silence after prayer" becomes an existential threat to God's coherence. This challenges notions of divine omnipotence and self-sufficiency, proposing instead that consciousness—even divine consciousness—requires external validation and repetition to maintain form. The poem grapples with questions of belief, linguistic reality, and whether anything truly exists independent of observation and naming. It suggests we don't discover God through prayer; we literally speak God into being, word by accumulated word.

Does God grow smaller when we cease to speak?
When altars cool to stone and tongues go slack,
When every throat that screamed for Him grows weak
And tips its empty bowl of pleading back?

Divinity requires our repetition—
The asking shapes what otherwise would thin
Past form, past center, just the vast attrition
Of being stretched past where a self begins.

The silence after prayer's where God dissolves:
No axis, only endless dark eclipse,
Consciousness unwound past what resolves—
God swallowing His own void through our lips.

    We think that heaven answers when we pray—
    But God's the sum of every word we say.

Krishna Patel
